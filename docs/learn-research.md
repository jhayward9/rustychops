# Learn: scale and practice research

Researched October 4, 2026. Research only; no application implementation changes.

## Scope and terminology

Scales are musical pitch collections, not guitar-specific objects. Guitar tuning and fingering determine how a scale is played. There is no universally exhaustive list of named scales: aliases, synthetic scales, cultural systems, and microtonal traditions extend beyond the usual twelve-tone guitar curriculum. This catalog covers common Western guitar material and identifies expansion families rather than claiming to contain every scale in existence.

Keep these separate in Learn: scale family/formula, tonic (key), neck position, fingering pattern, and exercise. A new box is not necessarily a new key. Starting on a different note does not by itself establish a new mode; the tonal center and musical context matter.

## Core scale catalog

Degrees are relative to the major scale on the same tonic. b = lowered a semitone, # = raised a semitone. The octave root is omitted.

| Scale | Degrees |
| --- | --- |
| Major pentatonic | 1 2 3 5 6 |
| Minor pentatonic | 1 b3 4 5 b7 |
| Major blues | 1 2 b3 3 5 6 |
| Minor blues | 1 b3 4 b5 5 b7 |
| Major / Ionian | 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 |
| Dorian | 1 2 b3 4 5 6 b7 |
| Phrygian | 1 b2 b3 4 5 b6 b7 |
| Lydian | 1 2 3 #4 5 6 7 |
| Mixolydian | 1 2 3 4 5 6 b7 |
| Natural minor / Aeolian | 1 2 b3 4 5 b6 b7 |
| Locrian | 1 b2 b3 4 b5 b6 b7 |
| Harmonic minor | 1 2 b3 4 5 b6 7 |
| Melodic minor (jazz/ascending form) | 1 2 b3 4 5 6 7 |
| Harmonic major | 1 2 3 4 5 b6 7 |
| Double harmonic major | 1 b2 3 4 5 b6 7 |
| Whole tone | 1 2 3 #4 #5 b7 |
| Diminished, whole-half | 1 2 b3 4 b5 b6 6 7 |
| Diminished, half-whole | 1 b2 b3 3 #4 5 6 b7 |
| Augmented (one common form) | 1 #2 3 5 #5 7 |
| Chromatic | All twelve semitones |

Classical melodic minor commonly uses raised 6 and 7 ascending and natural minor descending. Jazz melodic minor uses the raised form in both directions. Learn must distinguish these explicitly.

### Extended modal families

- Harmonic minor modes: harmonic minor; Locrian natural 6; Ionian #5; Dorian #4; Phrygian dominant; Lydian #2; ultralocrian (also called super Locrian bb7). Names vary, so formulas should be authoritative.
- Melodic minor modes: melodic minor; Dorian b2; Lydian augmented; Lydian dominant; Mixolydian b6; Locrian natural 2; altered / super Locrian.
- Harmonic major modes: harmonic major; Dorian b5; Phrygian b4; Lydian b3; Mixolydian b2; Lydian augmented #2; Locrian bb7.
- Double harmonic modes include Hungarian minor (1 2 b3 #4 5 b6 7). Other modes have inconsistent names; identify them by parent and degree until individually verified.

### Additional catalog candidates

- Bebop scales: dominant (Mixolydian plus 7), major (major plus #5/b6), and minor variants. "Minor bebop" has multiple definitions; avoid a single ambiguous entry.
- Other pentatonics: suspended (1 2 4 5 b7), dominant (1 2 3 5 b7), minor-sixth (1 b3 4 5 6), and named regional collections such as Hirajoshi, In, Insen, Iwato, and Kumoi. Verify each definition and cultural description before adding lessons.
- Further synthetic families: Neapolitan major/minor, Hungarian major, enigmatic, Prometheus, and Messiaen modes of limited transposition.
- Regional traditions cannot be represented faithfully by pitch formulas alone. Ragas and maqamat may require characteristic movement, ornaments, tuning, and intonation beyond equal-tempered frets.

The extended entries are expansion candidates, not a fully verified lesson syllabus. Do not equate aliases with extra unique scales. Arpeggios and chromatic finger drills belong in separate exercise categories.

## The user's fret-by-fret pentatonic routine

Assumption: standard E A D G B E tuning, standard minor pentatonic box 1, lowest root on string 6 under the first finger. Without that assumption, the starting fret alone does not determine the key.

| Low-E starting/root fret | Minor pentatonic tonic |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | F |
| 2 | F# / Gb |
| 3 | G |
| 4 | G# / Ab |
| 5 | A |
| 6 | A# / Bb |
| 7 | B |
| 8 | C |
| 9 | C# / Db |
| 10 | D |
| 11 | D# / Eb |
| 12 | E |

This covers all twelve tonic pitch classes. The E scale at fret 12 is an octave above the open-position E scale. The F shape repeats at fret 13.

For root fret r, ascending string order is low E, A, D, G, B, high E. Play these fret pairs:

| String | Frets |
| --- | --- |
| Low E | r, r+3 |
| A | r, r+2 |
| D | r, r+2 |
| G | r, r+2 |
| B | r, r+3 |
| High E | r, r+3 |

Reverse the note order to descend. This is twelve played notes ascending, with repeated pitch classes in multiple octaves. At r=1 the pairs are 1–4, 1–3, 1–3, 1–3, 1–4, 1–4; at r=2 add one to each fret; at r=3 add two. The root-fret range 1–12 requires playable frets through 15.

Major pentatonic is a different fingering when rooted on the same low-E note. Reusing a minor box produces its relative major collection, not the parallel major on that same root. For example, F minor pentatonic and Ab major pentatonic share pitches.

## Practice routines

The following are proposed app routines synthesized from the teaching sources, not verbatim published programs. Suggested tempos and repetition thresholds are product defaults to adjust, not universal rules.

1. **Chromatic key ladder — the requested routine.** Start box 1 at fret 1. Ascend and descend once or a configurable number of times, then shift the whole box one fret. Continue through root fret 12. Optional return journey: 12 down to 1. Display the current tonic and next root fret. Define whether the turnaround note is repeated and whether shifts include a rest/count-in.
2. **Circle of fourths.** Play the same exercise in F, Bb, Eb, Ab, Db, Gb, B, E, A, D, G, C. Root frets are 1, 6, 11, 4, 9, 2, 7, 12, 5, 10, 3, 8. This requires deliberate root finding instead of automatic adjacent shifts. Reversing the cycle gives fifths.
3. **Random keys.** Shuffle all twelve tonics without replacement. Find the root, name it, then ascend and descend. Reveal the fret as a hint rather than always supplying it.
4. **Five boxes, one key.** Keep A minor fixed and practice its five pentatonic patterns, then connect adjacent patterns. This teaches neck coverage rather than transposition. Pattern numbers differ across teaching systems, so diagrams and root markers must identify them.
5. **One string, all keys.** Play a root-to-octave pentatonic scale along one string, reverse, and transpose. This exposes interval spacing instead of relying exclusively on boxes. Fret limits may require octave repositioning.
6. **Sequences across keys.** Replace straight runs with groups of three/four or interval sequences, then apply the same sequence to every tonic. Distinguish three-note sequences from triplet rhythm.
7. **Musical application.** Follow a drill with short phrases over a matching drone or backing track; resolve to the tonic and use pauses. A multi-key drill should change accompaniment with the key.

Suggested first session: 1 minute identifying roots, 6 minutes of the chromatic ladder at a comfortable tempo, 2 minutes repeating difficult shifts, and 3 minutes making phrases in one chosen key. Begin around 60 BPM with one note per click if comfortable, use even alternate picking, and raise tempo only after consistent clean repetitions. Offer two notes per click separately from the BPM control. The whole twelve-key run may take longer at slow tempos; allow partial sessions and resume.

## Implications for Learn (proposal, not implementation)

- Main navigation entry: Learn.
- Initial lessons: minor pentatonic, major pentatonic, blues, major, natural minor, then modes and advanced families.
- Independent choices for tonic, pattern, tuning, fret range, direction, key order, repetitions, tempo, and notes per beat.
- First featured routine: minor pentatonic box 1, root frets 1–12, ascending and descending.
- Show fretboard roots, intervals/notes, fingering, and tablature. Explain why the tonic changes when a movable box shifts.
- Keep "transpose through keys" and "connect positions in one key" visibly distinct.
- Store formulas independently of pattern definitions. Aliases point to one formula where appropriate; direction-dependent scales need separate ascent/descent definitions.
- Account for open-string fingerings and available fret count. Do not silently crop a scale when a pattern extends beyond the selected range.
- Completion initially means the user completed a guided routine; accurate live pitch/rhythm scoring would need separate validation work.

## Additional research: absolute beginner to virtuoso-style lead playing

Researched October 4, 2026 using providers' public curriculum pages. This evaluates curriculum coverage, not independently measured student outcomes. Learning selected Vai/Malmsteen-style licks is a narrower goal than attaining either artist's overall musicianship. None of the reviewed pages establishes a guaranteed zero-to-virtuoso result or timetable.

### Proposed Learn progression

This is an original curriculum synthesis inspired by the reviewed structures. Use original exercises and explanations; external course content is reference material.

| Stage | Skills and lesson content | Example readiness evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 — First contact | Guitar parts, tuning, string/fret numbering, comfortable setup, holding a pick, fretting a single note, reading a simple tab | Tune with assistance, find a requested string/fret and play clear single notes |
| 1 — First music | Simple riffs, open chords, chord changes, rests, pulse, strumming, basic muting | Play a short riff and chord song in steady time |
| 2 — Reliable foundations | Power/barre chords, subdivisions, alternate picking, low-E/A root notes, minor pentatonic, basic bends and vibrato | Play a full simple piece, keep a steady pulse and complete a slow scale without unwanted ringing |
| 3 — Developing lead player | Five pentatonic boxes, major/natural minor, twelve-key routines, hammer-ons/pull-offs, slides, phrasing, intervals and ear training | Transpose a phrase, connect two positions and improvise short phrases over a matching backing track |
| 4 — Advanced foundation | Three-note-per-string patterns, modal sound over harmony, triads/arpeggios, string crossing/skipping, accurate bends, two-hand synchronization | Play contrasting picked and legato passages cleanly at a recorded personal tempo; explain their roots and chord context |
| 5A — Expressive rock / Vai-inspired | Fluid legato, tapping, wide intervals, rhythmic phrasing, harmonics, controlled vibrato and whammy work where hardware permits | Perform an original expressive etude with accurate pitch, controlled noise and clear phrasing |
| 5B — Neoclassical / Malmsteen-inspired | Harmonic minor, Phrygian dominant, diminished vocabulary, pedal tones, sequential runs, alternate/economy picking and sweep arpeggios | Perform an original neoclassical etude with clean string transitions and intentional harmonic resolution |
| 6 — Virtuoso-style application | Longer pieces, fast phrases in musical context, endurance, dynamic control, improvisation, transcription and composition | Record a complete demanding performance and assess timing, articulation, pitch, noise, expression and consistency |

Branches are not exclusive. Keep rhythm, ear training, theory, repertoire and creativity active through every stage. Avoid reducing the final goal to one BPM threshold or a scale-speed test. Readiness examples are proposed assessment criteria, not published provider requirements.

### Practice and product design conclusions

- Offer a guided path from first contact, plus a placement check for returning players.
- Each lesson needs a clear skill, demonstration, original tab/exercise, slow practice loop, application in music and a readiness check.
- Separate watching a lesson, completing a practice session and demonstrating proficiency. User self-assessment can support the initial version; automated proficiency claims require validated scoring.
- Progress by clean, repeatable playing at the learner's tempo. Increase speed gradually; include varied rhythms, short difficult fragments, full phrases and musical use.
- Introduce basic legato, bends and muting early; reserve demanding combinations and high-speed execution for later prerequisites.
- The requested twelve-key pentatonic ladder fits Stage 3 after the learner understands one movable box and its root. Offer small ranges first, then frets 1–12.
- Provide Vai-inspired and Malmsteen-inspired original exercises as advanced branches. Licensed song transcriptions or direct course reproduction would require appropriate content rights.
- Treat commercial labels such as "shred in six weeks" as course packaging, not a mastery deadline. Do not promise a fixed completion date for virtuoso ability.
